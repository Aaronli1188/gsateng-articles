TOPIC: The practice of keeping a handwritten index of films watched at home with short personal notes TYPE: C

Sara pulled the small notebook from the shelf and opened it to a fresh page. She wrote the date, the film title, and underlined it twice. Around her, the living room was quiet. The lamp made a warm circle of light on the table.

She had started the notebook two years ago after forgetting a film she had loved. She could remember the feeling — a kind of soft sadness — but not the name. That loss bothered her more than it should have. So she began writing one line after each film: not a review, just a word or two about how it left her feeling. *Slow. Heavy. Like rain.* Some entries were only four words long.

One evening she flipped back through the old pages while waiting for something to heat on the stove. She did not expect much. But she found herself reading every entry, surprised by who she had been two winters ago — what had moved her, what she had found funny, what she had watched twice without writing why.

The notebook was not a record of films. She saw that now. It was a record of evenings, each one already gone, held in a few small words.
