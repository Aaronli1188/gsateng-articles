You might see a small fly trapped inside one of the green mouths tomorrow. If this happens, do not touch the leaves or try to pull the insect out. The plant will digest its food slowly over several days. Occasionally a trap stays closed for a week, which is perfectly normal.

The science of why certain smells can trigger vivid and emotional childhood memories

Imagine you are walking past a bakery and the scent of fresh bread fills your nose. Suddenly, you feel like you are six years old again in your grandmother's kitchen. Why does a simple smell have the power to bring back such strong feelings from the past? This experience is actually a special trick of the human brain.

When we breathe in an aroma, the signal goes directly to the parts of the brain that handle emotions. Most other senses, like sight or hearing, must pass through several checkpoints before reaching these areas. Smell is the only sense with such a fast-track connection to our memory centers. Research shows that people can remember a specific scent with 65 percent accuracy even after one whole year.

Surprisingly, odors can trigger "time travel" more effectively than looking at old family photos. While a picture reminds us of what happened, a smell makes us feel the exact emotion we felt at that moment. Scientists have discovered that these scent-memories are usually formed before a child reaches the age of ten. This is why certain soaps often remind adults of their very first classroom.

Next time you visit a new place, pay close attention to how it smells. You might be creating a "scent bookmark" that your brain will save for decades. Years later, a similar odor could suddenly transport you back to this very afternoon. What smells from your life right now will become the keys to your future memories?

An explanation of how "smart glass" changes its transparency using electrical signals

Have you ever walked past a modern office and seen the windows turn cloudy in one second? This magic trick happens because of a special material called smart glass. It looks like a normal window, but it hides what is inside without using any curtains.

The secret is a thin layer of liquid crystals hidden between two sheets of glass. When you flip a switch, an electrical signal moves through the crystals and makes them line up. This allows light to pass through, so the glass becomes clear. Without power, these tiny particles face different directions and block the view. Usually, this process takes less than one second.

Most people think this glass needs a lot of energy to stay clear, but that is not always true. Some newer versions only use electricity to change their state, which saves power over time. Interestingly, this technology can also block heat from the sun while letting light in. This keeps rooms cool during hot summers without turning off the lights.

We already see these windows on airplanes and in hospitals to give patients privacy. In the future, your phone screen or your glasses might use this technology to protect your eyes. Imagine walking through a city where every building changes color to match the morning sky.

How vertical farming technology allows crops to grow in the middle of crowded skyscrapers

Imagine walking past a glass skyscraper in Taipei and seeing rows of bright green lettuce instead of office desks. How can vegetables grow inside a building without any soil or sunlight? This is the reality of vertical farming, where plants reach for the ceiling instead of the sky.

Indoor farms use layers of shelves that go up many meters high. Instead of the sun, special LED lights provide exactly what the plants need to grow quickly. Scientists have developed water systems that feed the roots with minerals. This method uses 90% less water than traditional farming because the liquid is recycled.

Most people think these farms are only for small salads, but some buildings now grow strawberries and even small fruit trees. A surprising benefit is that farmers do not need to use any chemicals to kill insects. Since the environment is completely closed, bugs cannot get inside. This means the food is often cleaner than what comes from a field.

Having these farms in the city center means that a sandwich shop downstairs can get fresh spinach in minutes. It reduces the need for large trucks to drive long distances from the countryside. As more people move into crowded cities, our dinner might soon come from the tall building just across the street.

An opinion piece arguing that summer vacation should be replaced by more frequent short breaks

Imagine a student sitting at home in August, feeling bored because the two-month break is far too long. I believe that schools should replace the traditional summer vacation with several shorter breaks throughout the academic year. This change would help everyone stay focused and prevent students from forgetting important lessons.

Long holidays often lead to "learning loss," where students forget English vocabulary or math formulas during the hot July and August months. If we have two-week breaks every few months instead, teachers will not need to spend the first month of every semester reviewing old material. For instance, a student who takes a short break in October returns to class with a fresh mind but still remembers their recent science projects.

Some people might argue that families need long summers for international travel or special summer camps. It is true that planning a big trip is easier when you have eight weeks of free time. However, many parents work during the summer and find it difficult to arrange childcare for such a long period every year.

Frequent breaks create a better rhythm for learning and resting without losing momentum. The traditional school calendar was designed for an old way of life that might not fit our modern needs anymore. You can decide if you prefer one long wait for summer or a year that offers regular moments of rest. Perhaps you can picture yourself enjoying a week off in the middle of a rainy November.

A critique of a popular new board game designed to teach teenagers financial literacy

The new board game "Wealth Race" is a boring waste of time for teenagers who want to learn about money. Although the box looks colorful and exciting, the gameplay feels like a long math lesson rather than a fun activity. I felt very disappointed after playing it with my classmates for two hours.

This game’s biggest problem is that the rules are far too repetitive. For instance, players spend every turn rolling dice and paying taxes or rent without making any real choices. You just move your plastic token around the board and wait for your cash to disappear.

Of course, the game does teach players how to calculate basic interest and manage a small budget. Some people might find the colorful cards and paper money helpful for understanding how banks work in the real world. However, these small lessons are hidden behind many rules that are too difficult to follow.

I still believe that there are better ways to learn about financial literacy than playing "Wealth Race." A simple banking app or a trip to the supermarket would offer a much better experience for young people. Is it worth spending your Saturday afternoon doing math problems on a board?
